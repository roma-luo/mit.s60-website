# Final Project Idea 2: Braindance — Sellable Agent Memories of Lived Expertise

> *Inspired by braindance in Cyberpunk 2077: a recording of someone's lived experience that another person can replay and feel. What if an agent's memory of an expert's way of thinking could be "replayed" the same way?*

**Who it is for:** Learners who want to absorb not just an expert's knowledge, but their *judgment* — how a senior doctor notices danger signs, how a good investor decides to walk away, how an experienced programmer smells a bad architecture.

**The problem:** Today, expertise transfer is limited to content: books, courses, videos. But what makes an expert valuable is mostly tacit — thousands of small decisions and instincts that never get written down. Meanwhile, AI agents are starting to accumulate exactly this: long memories of working alongside a person, observing their decisions and their outcomes. That memory is an asset — and right now it belongs to platforms, dies when you switch tools, and can never be shared.

**How an agent could help:** An agent works alongside an expert for months and builds a structured memory: situations observed, decisions made, reasoning given, outcomes that followed. This memory is packaged into a portable, sellable "experience file." A learner's agent loads it and can then *replay* the expert's judgment — "here is a situation like one your mentor faced; here is what they noticed, what they decided, and what happened." The buyer doesn't get raw private data; they get distilled patterns of thinking.

**What it augments:** Learning and intuition — the slowest human faculties to develop. Expertise that today takes 10 years of apprenticeship could be partially transferred. It also raises the ownership question that makes this a cognitive-augmentation issue: if memory is an extension of the mind, agent memory should belong to the person who lived it, and be theirs to keep, carry, or sell.
